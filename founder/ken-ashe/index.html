Operating discipline

Why an accountant and project manager runs a domain and SEO practice

Most domain deals and SEO engagements fail for unglamorous reasons. Nobody wrote down what "done" means. The valuation was a gut feeling. The reporting measured activity instead of outcomes. Ken Ashe started Lucky Domains to run this work the way regulated, high-stakes work is supposed to run.

Ken's background is in accounting and finance. He is a licensed Certified Public Accountant in New Jersey and a Chartered Global Management Accountant, with a career that began in financial reporting before moving into project, product, and program leadership, including at Prudential Financial. He holds the Project Management Professional and PMI Agile Certified Practitioner certifications from the Project Management Institute. That is roughly two decades of being accountable for numbers other people relied on and for delivery dates other people planned around.

Those habits are the operating system of Lucky Domains.

Valuation, not vibes

A domain acquisition is a capital purchase. Ken builds a fair-market range from comparable sales, traffic and keyword data, and the domain's strategic value to your business, then negotiates against that number rather than against the seller's opening ask.

Scope, controls, escrow

Every engagement starts with a written scope and a change process. Every transfer runs through third-party escrow so funds release only when the domain is verifiably in your control. It is the same segregation-of-duties thinking a CPA applies to cash.

Reporting you can question

SEO reporting at Lucky Domains ties rankings and traffic back to leads and revenue, states its assumptions, and is honest about what did not work. If a report cannot survive a skeptical review, it is not finished.

Discretion is the other half of the job. Ken approaches domain owners without revealing who the buyer is, because a price should reflect what an asset is worth, not how badly a well-funded buyer appears to want it. He also represents sellers who want a confidential, well-managed exit from a domain they no longer need.

Practical AI

Building the tools that run the practice

Ken's day-to-day work is building AI applications and automated workflows at the application layer: combining existing models, APIs, coding tools, and automation platforms into agents, automations, and AI-assisted websites, rather than training models or building infrastructure. He documents that work in public at kenashe.ai, including what shipped, what broke, and what the evidence supports.

Lucky Domains is where that work meets a real business. The same disciplines show up in three places:

  • Automated domain research. Ken applies the same scripted, rules-based approach to domain research: watching expiring and backordered domains, pulling ownership and history signals, and scoring candidates against a client's brief, so the shortlist you see has already been filtered by rules you can read.
  • AI-assisted technical SEO. Crawl audits, structured data validation, canonical and duplicate-URL checks, and content briefs built from real search demand. AI does the volume; Ken owns the judgment and signs off on every recommendation.
  • Websites shipped end to end. This site, and kenashe.ai, were designed and shipped with AI coding tools, from the first prompt to a live, search-ready site with clean structured data. Lucky Domains applies that build process to client rebrands that need a new name and a new site at the same time.

The point is not to replace expertise with automation. It is to make the expert work reproducible, testable, and honest. A CPA would call that a control. A project manager would call it a repeatable process. Ken calls it the only sensible way to run an agency in 2026.

You can follow the builds themselves, including the project log and the autonomous systems Ken runs under his own name, at kenashe.ai/building.
