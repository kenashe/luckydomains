Background

Ken builds things, tests new technology, and publishes what actually happened. Lucky Domains is the company that operates what gets built.

He started his career in accounting and financial reporting before moving into project, product, and program leadership. Over roughly two decades, that career ran through S&P Global, EY, Noom, and Prudential Financial, where he served as Director of Project and Product Management.

He built Lucky Domains alongside that work and still runs it himself. He later left corporate work to focus on entrepreneurship and building practical AI applications: agents, automations, and internet-native services, with the evidence published, failures included. His public focus areas include AI agents, Bitcoin, digital identity, and domain names.

The credentials support the work rather than define it. A CPA's instinct for controls and a project manager's delivery discipline show up in how the ventures are built: written specifications, spending limits, independent reviews, and limits stated plainly. The same habits run the domain desk: a valuation before outreach, a written scope, and third-party escrow.

A CPA would call that a control. A project manager would call it a repeatable process. I call it the only sensible way to run this practice.
