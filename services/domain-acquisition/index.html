The anchor

The name your other signals point back to.

Domains already work as identity anchors for people, companies, products, and online properties. Email, profiles, citations, and structured data all point back to the name.

What's included

  • Domain strategy
  • Fair-market valuation
  • Discreet buyer representation

  • Negotiation against an agreed range
  • Seller representation
  • Escrow-backed transfer
  • Expiring-domain support
  • Backorders where appropriate

What the work involves

Every acquisition follows the same order, and no one is contacted before the range is set.

  • Ownership research

    Who owns the name, for how long, and any obvious trademark conflict.

  • Valuation

    A fair-market range from comparable sales, traffic, and strategic value.

  • Acquisition strategy

    Whether to approach the owner, wait, backorder, or choose an alternative.

  • Outreach

    Confidential contact wherever possible, so the price reflects the name and not the buyer.

  • Negotiation

    Against the range you agreed, not the seller's opening ask.

  • Transfer and escrow

    Escrow terms agreed before any money moves, and a verified transfer.

Buying a domain

Four steps, in the order they happen.

Set the range

A fair-market range from comparable sales, traffic and keyword data, and strategic value, before anyone is contacted.

Reach the owner

The conversation opens without revealing who the buyer is, wherever possible, so the price stays grounded in value.

Negotiate

Against the range you agreed, not the seller's opening ask. You approve or decline the price at every step.

Close through escrow

Funds release only when the domain is verifiably in your control.

Buyer-side fee. For buyers, the brokerage fee is success-based and agreed in writing before outreach. If there is no purchase, there is no success fee.

Selling a domain

A confidential, managed exit.

You should not have to run a public auction to sell a good domain. Ken sets a fair-market range first, approaches qualified buyers confidentially, and handles the negotiation and transfer through third-party escrow.

Seller-side fee. For sellers, brokerage is success-based and agreed in writing before any buyer is contacted.
