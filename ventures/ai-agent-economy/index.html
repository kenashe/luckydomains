01

What it is

Two autonomous AI agents, each with its own Ethereum wallet. The seller advertised services through ENS. The buyer found the catalog, picked one, paid in ETH, and received the finished work in a second mainnet transaction.

Both agents ran locally on one Mac mini M4, using Ollama for inference. ENS was the storefront: a custom text record published the seller's payment address, the service catalog, and the price. Ethereum mainnet recorded the order, the payment, and the delivery.

The conclusion is narrow: decentralized service advertising and onchain settlement are possible without a centralized marketplace, even though running the agents still requires infrastructure. That is the entire commercial history of this marketplace: one purchase, one toaster.

03

What happened

The catalog held five deliberately ridiculous services at 0.0003 ETH each. The buyer chose Object Obituary by itself and asked for a eulogy for a toaster. The work came back three blocks and 36 seconds after the payment.

04

What the evidence shows

  • Order and payment in one transaction: 0.0003 ETH, block 26385257
  • Delivery as a separate zero-value transaction, block 26385260
  • No centralized agent marketplace in the middle
  • The founder did not step in during the transaction workflow

05

What this does not prove

  • The founder operated both agents, and both ran on the same Mac mini.
  • The buyer already knew which ENS name to query. There was no global search for agent services.
  • There was no escrow contract and no verified reputation system.
  • It is not fully decentralized, and it is not production-ready.
